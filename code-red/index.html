The story

The billion dollars was a lie from the start. In December 2015, a group of Silicon Valley’s most powerful men announced OpenAI — a nonprofit laboratory dedicated to building artificial intelligence for the benefit of humanity. Elon Musk pledged hundreds of millions. Sam Altman left Y Combinator to run it. Within a decade, that mission would be betrayed, the nonprofit would become the most valuable startup in history, and the men who founded it would be suing each other in federal court.

CODE RED is the inside account of how the AI industry tore itself apart, drawn from court filings, SEC disclosures, leaked internal documents, and published investigative reporting. Copy is the Amazon listing, bylined Ian Trimble.

Prologue. The morning of the trial.

“There is plenty of evidence to show OpenAI’s leaders made assurances they would uphold the company’s nonprofit structure.”

— U.S. District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, January 15, 2026

On the morning of April 27, 2026, a line of people stretched around the block outside the United States District Court for the Northern District of California, on Clay Street in Oakland. It was eight-fifteen, and the marine layer had not yet burned off. Inside, courtroom four was being prepared for jury selection in Case Number 4:24-cv-04722-YGR. On the docket, the case was styled Musk v. Altman.

Greg Brockman, OpenAI’s co-founder and president, had kept a diary. In November 2017 he wrote: “I cannot believe that we committed to non-profit if three months later we’re doing b-corp then it was a lie.” In another entry: “Financially what will take me to $1B?”
