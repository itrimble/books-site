1,200 passengers. No signal. No shore. No rules.

The Wi-Fi dies first. Then the engines. Then the stories people tell themselves about who they are when rescue is not coming.

A next-generation cruise ship drifts silent in the Caribbean while a hidden behavioral experiment turns a floating resort into a pressure cooker of power, hunger, surveillance, and choice.

A ship designed for pleasure becomes a lab for the end of civilization.

The MV Elysium leaves Port Canaveral as a floating city of bottomless cocktails, influencer content, luxury suites, and algorithmic convenience. By Day Two, it is invisible to the world.

“Athena sees everything. The ship was never lost. It was selected.”

— from the world of DEAD SIGNAL

For readers who like their thrillers sharp, plausible, and morally nasty.

Think premium-cruise spectacle colliding with hard-sci-fi plausibility: disabled comms, GPS spoofing, ration math, heat injury, shipboard politics, influencer identity collapse, and an AI-backed experiment hiding behind hospitality software.

🛰️ No rescue signal

Satellite, AIS, EPIRB, SART, and Wi-Fi failures are grounded in real maritime systems and plausible sabotage paths.

🥃 Luxury turns feral

Open bars and infinity pools give way to heat, ration lines, faction politics, and impossible triage.

👁️ Everyone is watched

Athena, the shipboard AI concierge, starts as convenience and becomes the surveillance harness.

📹 Content as evidence

Marcus Cole boards to film a budget-cruise video. His footage may become the trial record of the century.

⚕️ Human limits

Nina Park, an ER nurse on her first solo vacation in years, becomes the medical authority no one can afford to lose.

⚓ Authority corrodes

Wade Haskell knows how to impose order. The question is when order becomes occupation.
