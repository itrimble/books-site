Prologue: The Silicon Dirge

The city didn’t sleep; it just entered a low-power state and dreamt of better days.

The Bay Enclave was a pressure cooker of steam and silicon. Following the 2039 Kinetic Strikes, the local geothermal vents had ruptured, turning the lower levels into a permanent sauna. Now, Living Intelligence grids—AI systems fused with biological sensors—governed the city’s heartbeat, from the oxygen mix in the sumps to the high-frequency trading in the towers.

Down in the “sumps,” where the fog was thick enough to chew on, the air tasted of ozone and recycled hope. Up in the glass towers of the Financial District, the elite played God with algorithms. But in the spaces between—the dark alleys of Blackwood Row and the rotting piers of the industrial sector—that’s where the real history was written.

Silas Vane sat in the center of his web, surrounded by the hum of cooling fans and the rhythmic clicking of a mechanical music box. He watched the screen of his primary terminal as the Eidolon Core pulsed with a cold, blue light. It was a beautiful monster, a two-faced god that could peel back the secrets of the world.

Vane knew they were coming. He reached into his pocket and touched the amber Halcyon Key, the only thing that could silence the monster he had birthed.

“The song,” he whispered to the empty room. “It always ends with the song.”

He pressed a key, and for a fleeting moment, the sound of a smoky, melancholic voice filled the warehouse, singing of a scarlet lark in a cage. Then the power died, the screens went black, and Silas Vane walked out into the fog to meet his ghost.

Chapter 1: The Uninvited Echo

The rain wasn’t just falling; it was auditioning for a disaster movie. Each drop hit my third-floor office window on Blackwood Row with the suicidal enthusiasm of a tiny, waterlogged kamikaze pilot.

Outside, the perpetual gloom of the Bay Enclave, 2043, was amplified by the downpour, neon signs bleeding fuzzy halos onto the slick, cracked pavement below. Inside, the Mercer Detective Agency Global Headquarters and Occasional Nap Zone was a testament to dashed hopes. The rent was overdue. My bank account, if it could make a sound, would produce an echo.

That’s when the vid-phone on my desk gave a startled chirp. Not the usual chime I’d programmed for the bill collectors. This was a short, sharp, encrypted burst — the digital equivalent of a shadowy figure hissing ‘pst!’ from a dark alley.

The screen resolved into a heavily pixelated face. “Mercer? Max Mercer?” The voice was a synthesized baritone, digitally flattened.

“Depends who’s asking,” I said. “And more importantly, who’s paying.”

“Let’s just say I’m a… concerned party. Regarding a mutual acquaintance. Silas Vane.”

Vane. Tech-head extraordinaire, certified genius, certifiable recluse. Last I heard he was holed up south of Market, building things nobody should control.

“He’s gone. Vanished.” The synthesized voice didn’t betray a hint of emotion, but the word hung in the stale office air. “There are twenty thousand credits in it for you. Find Vane. Or, at the very least, secure that chip before the wrong people do.”
